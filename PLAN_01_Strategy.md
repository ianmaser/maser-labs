2. Positioning & Audience
   Primary Audience: Local small businesses
   Non-technical, risk-averse, outcome-focused
   Fears: cost, being oversold, not understanding what they're paying for
   Wants: more customers, less manual work, professional web presence, someone trustworthy
   Language: plain English, outcomes not tech ("save hours a week," not "workflow automation pipeline")
   Secondary Audience: Startups / tech founders
   Semi-technical to technical, capability-focused
   Wants: proof of real projects, modern stack, speed, AI fluency
   Language: can handle "Next.js, Supabase, Claude API" — wants the receipts
   The Balancing Principle
   Lead every section with plain-English outcomes; back it up with technical proof further down. The local owner reads the top; the founder scrolls for substance. Never make the local owner feel dumb; never make the founder feel it's fluff.
   Brand vs. Personal — RESOLVED: Personal-forward
   Your competitive edge with locals is that you're NOT a faceless agency — you're a senior engineer giving agency-quality work with a real human they can talk to
   Citibank/Verizon credibility is personal equity — must be visible
   "Maser Labs" keeps it personal but with room to grow into a studio brand later
   Sequence: start personal → convert to agency name only if/when demand forces hiring

4. Design Direction — LOCKED (synthesized from 6 references)
   Core principle: the site IS the portfolio. Its quality is the single most persuasive sales argument. Target reaction: "Wow — I could have something even half this good?"
   The Synthesized Visual Identity
   Base: near-black background (deep charcoal/midnight navy → black). NOT flat — depth via layered radial glows, gradient section transitions, subtle vignetting.
   Accent palette: cyan / electric-blue + purple/violet, living in glows, gradients, and CTAs — never in body text. ("Maser beam / laser precision" energy. Coherent with EDGE & Hold or Fold.)
   Palette travels: can shift warm-to-cool or purple→cyan across scroll (per Ref 1) for a dynamic feel.
   One hero showpiece: a single intricate, glowing, futuristic/sci-fi centerpiece graphic = the primary "wow." Everything else stays calm/restrained. The restraint is what makes the wow land (selective wow, not wow-everywhere).
   Depth technique (key, repeatable): soft radial glows positioned behind key elements (hero, portfolio pieces, CTA sections) so content feels "lit from within."
   Typography: big, confident, clean white sans headlines (Inter / Geist or similar); muted gray body copy. Color stays in glows/graphics, text stays clean & readable.
   Product/portfolio framing: portfolio pieces (EDGE, Hold or Fold) shown in dark UI frames, optionally angled/tilted with a glow behind — dynamic, premium.
   Ghosted giant wordmark: huge faded "MASER LABS" in footer/background area. High polish, near-zero effort.
   Section patterns confirmed by refs: pain-point "Problems We Solve" style section → then Maser Labs as the solution; stats block with bold real numbers; trust/logo strip under hero; mission/vision split with glowing center mark.
   Hero Centerpiece — Build Decision (IMPORTANT, carry to build doc)
   Achieve the "wow" via a lightweight approach, NOT a heavy live WebGL/Three.js particle rig (performance + mobile risk, over-scoped):

Default: static/generated hero graphic + layered CSS motion (breathing glows, gradient drift, subtle parallax).
Acceptable: a heavily-compressed hero video (ref clip was ~19MB → must optimize hard).
Avoid: full live particle systems. Keep the wow, protect the speed.
Motion
Scroll-triggered reveals, subtle parallax, smooth transitions, tasteful micro-interactions, breathing glow accents. Impress without being a demo reel. 2-3 genuine "wow" moments; calm confidence everywhere else.
Non-negotiables
Speed. Gorgeous + slow contradicts "I build things right." Optimize images (webp), lazy-load, strong performance scores. Fast + beautiful = the whole proof.
Mobile-first. Ad/local-search traffic is heavily mobile. Flawless on phone first, desktop as enhancement.
Accessibility. Clean contrast (neon never on paragraph text), semantic structure — also helps SEO.
Logo
Wordmark-based ("Maser Labs" clean type) + small geometric mark hinting at precision/energy/a beam. Simple.

5. Pricing Philosophy — RESOLVED: Fully Hidden
   Model: Contact-for-quote, NO published prices.

Why (Ian's reasoning, endorsed):

Maximum flexibility — every client's needs are unique; don't pigeonhole price before hearing scope
Enables consultative selling & upselling — bundling, anchoring ("normally X, but bundled Z"), spotting add-on opportunities (e.g. "your site has low traffic — I can add an SEO suite, normally $\_\_\_, special today...")
Price anxiety is mitigable via good salesmanship + the free consult

Critical guardrail — de-risk the CONVERSATION so hidden pricing doesn't cause pre-call drop-off:

CTA is always "Free, no-pressure consult" / "Free 15-min discovery call" — NOT "Get a Quote"
Include a soft range signal (see §3.8) so anxious-but-serious buyers know you're in a normal range
Chatbot fields "roughly how much?" conversationally, warming leads toward booking 24/7

Internal pricing ranges (NOT published — for Ian's own quoting reference):

Starter site (SPA, few sections, contact form, mobile, basic SEO — e.g. ice cream shop tier): $1,500–3,000
Growth site (multi-page, CMS/admin, blog, stronger SEO, custom design — e.g. real estate tier): $3,500–7,000
AI automation add-on (chatbot, workflow automation): $1,000–3,000
Full custom app / dashboard / E2E build: $8,000+
Ongoing (maintenance, hosting, updates, SEO retainer): $150–500/mo recurring ← the quiet money
Note: price at the LOWER end for first few paid jobs (no direct reviews yet), raise as testimonials accumulate
Don't underprice to win — low prices attract bad clients and signal low quality. Local buyers compare you to $5-15k agencies, not $15/hr offshore.

7. SEO Strategy
   Two purposes: (1) traffic to own site, (2) a skill practiced here then sold as a service.

On-page: meta titles/descriptions, semantic HTML, header hierarchy, fast load, mobile-first, schema markup (LocalBusiness), sitemap, robots.txt
Local SEO: Google Business Profile, local keywords ("web developer [city]," "AI automation for small business [city]"), NAP consistency
Content: simple blog/guides section to rank long-tail + demonstrate expertise (also feeds lead-gen)
Honest expectation: SEO compounds over MONTHS, not days. Long-game channel + learnable/sellable service — not an immediate lead source.
Strategic note: ranking your OWN site well is your proof-of-competence for selling SEO. (This is partly why "Maser" beats "Modern Solutions" — far easier to own in search.)
